# Reading decisions

## Orient before compressing

Identify the paper's question, original sections, important figures/tables/equations, and dependencies needed to understand them. Preserve the original order in the index; the suggested route can differ. A useful unit answers a question such as "Why measure these two compartments separately?" or "What assumption makes this proof work?" The route should emerge from the paper, not a mandatory experiment checklist.

The first authored unit should make real progress at the requested level. Do not fill every chapter with generic summaries. Mark unexpanded units and missing supplements distinctly. Indexing a unit is not evidence that it has been explained.

## Match the reader

| Level | Writing decisions |
| --- | --- |
| light | Define the object before using its technical name. Explain only prerequisites needed now. Use concrete examples and identify where an analogy stops matching the paper. |
| medium | Use established disciplinary terms without re-teaching all fundamentals. Explain unfamiliar mechanisms, controls, quantities or reasoning transitions. |
| deep | Discuss exact definitions, assumptions, design, controls, baselines and limitations. Do not add jargon merely to sound advanced. |

The levels share source claims, numerical results and uncertainty. Preserve specialist labels so the reader can recognize them in the paper. Explain genes/proteins, variables/units or algorithms according to their actual role in this paper. Do not silently change entities, direction of effects or study populations while simplifying.

Glossary entries can be incrementally authored per level. Domain background added by the agent must be identified as background, with an external source when needed to verify a specialized claim. Do not attribute external background to the paper.

## Read evidence, not only captions

For a selected figure or argument, connect:

- the question being tested;
- what was varied, measured or assumed;
- the observed result or inference step;
- the claim it supports and its practical limit.

Use only relevant steps. A proof needs different explanation than an animal intervention. Avoid translating every method into a universal claim that it "proves the mechanism."

Track original location at page, figure/panel, table, equation or section granularity as available. PDF page numbers should be explicitly distinguished from printed page numbers. Open the original figure before interpreting axes, signs, significance markers, microscopy or experimental groups. Text extraction can corrupt superscripts, minus signs and multi-column order.

Keep original evidence separate from the author's proposed mechanism and the agent's teaching model. Preserve correlations versus interventions, human versus animal/cell evidence, measured quantities versus inferred mechanisms, and a schematic versus experimental results. Use precise qualifications only where they matter to the selected passage.

If caption, plotted direction and body text disagree, identify the exact discrepancy and mark it unresolved. Do not silently select a convenient version. A missing supplement stays missing even if the body describes its result. Distinguish what was read in the body from figures that were visually inspected.

## Visual explanations

Use source-derived visual explanations where they help: reveal a pathway step by step, compare source experimental conditions, highlight an original panel, or place a claim next to its evidence. Native details, tables, inline SVG and the reader's scoped stepper are supported.

Base teaching visuals on the paper's originals and cite them. Label any abstracted mechanism, conceptual counts or qualitative arrows as a schematic. Do not invent numeric sliders, dose-response curves or simulations from a paper that did not supply the corresponding model/data. If a source figure is unreadable or missing, explain the gap instead of manufacturing replacement data.

ImageGen is user-opt-in and reference-based. Inspect the original, pass it to the available image-generation tool, preserve the original separately, and link the generated teaching image to its source. Exact plots, experimental bands and statistical markings should remain originals or faithful data-based renderings. A generated image is not new evidence.

## Language and continuation

An explicit output language wins; otherwise use the user's reading-request language. Keep the saved language for follow-ups that only paste a quotation or name a file. Do not infer a locale switch from the paper's language. Translate interface labels, explanatory content, titles and exported prompts together; leave source images and original identifiers intact.

The unit button asks for one incremental change, while the global button supplies context for a fresh conversation. The agent needs the actual files to continue. A project ID or skill name alone cannot recover inaccessible content. Preserve older explanation levels and valid reading context; explain substantive factual corrections briefly.
